# Punyakoti Taila — Design System

> *"Pure tradition delivered through a modern premium shopping experience."*

## Brand context

**Punyakoti Taila** is a premium Indian D2C brand of organic, cold-pressed (wood-pressed / *kachi ghani* / *chekku*) edible oils. The name borrows from **Punyakoti** — the legendary truthful cow of the Kannada folk ballad — and **Taila** (तैल), Sanskrit for oil. Together it evokes purity, dharma, and the quiet dignity of village craft.

The product line is rooted in South Indian household tradition but built for the contemporary urban kitchen. Oils are pressed slowly on wooden *ghanis*, unrefined, unfiltered, and bottled in dark glass to protect potency. Every label is a story of provenance: which farm, which press, which batch.

### Product lines

| SKU family       | Notes                                                         |
|------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Sesame (Til)     | Hero product. Hot-region staple, deep amber, nutty.           |
| Coconut          | Kerala-sourced, virgin, pale gold, solidifies below 24°C.     |
| Groundnut        | All-purpose cooking oil; warm, earthy.                        |
| Mustard          | Pungent, North & East Indian cuisine, deep yellow.            |
| Sunflower        | Light, neutral; for daily cooking.                            |
| Black Sesame     | Limited; ceremonial / Ayurvedic.                              |
| Castor (Eranda)  | Wellness range — hair/skin, not edible.                       |

### Audience

Indian millennials and Gen-Z urban households (Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Chennai, Hyderabad) who care about provenance, ingredient labels, and the wellness/Ayurveda revival. Also NRI households nostalgic for grandmother's kitchen.

### Sources

This system was built **from a written brief only**. No codebase, Figma, or visual assets were provided. All decisions — color, type, logo, illustrations, components — are original direction calibrated to the brief. Where placeholders exist (product photography, founder imagery), they're explicitly marked.

If you have an existing brand book, logo files, hero photography, or a Figma file — drop them in via Import and I'll re-thread the system to match.

## CONTENT FUNDAMENTALS

### Voice
**Quietly confident. Warm. Specific.** We sound like a knowledgeable family member, not a brand. We don't sell — we share. Where mass brands shout health claims, we describe process: *which* mill, *which* district, *which* harvest. Specificity *is* the marketing.

### Tone register
- **Reverent** when describing tradition (the press, the ballad, the village).
- **Plain** when describing product (oils, kitchens, food).
- **Practical** when transacting (cart, shipping, returns) — no flourish here.

### Person & address
- We say **you**, not "the customer". Never "we" alone — always "you and us".
- "Our oils" sometimes; "your kitchen" often.
- First person plural is reserved for the founder's story page; everywhere else the brand speaks in second person.

### Casing
- **Sentence case** for buttons, nav, all UI. *Not* Title Case. *Never* ALL CAPS except small eyebrow labels.
- Product names get title case (*Wood-Pressed Sesame Oil*).
- Sanskrit/regional terms italicized on first use: *kachi ghani*, *chekku*, *taila*.

### Punctuation & rhythm
- Em-dashes welcome — they slow you down.
- Short sentences. Then a longer one that breathes a little.
- Oxford comma always.
- Numerals: "1 litre", not "one litre". But "five generations", not "5".

### Emoji
**No emoji.** Anywhere. Not in product copy, not in transactional emails, not on social. The brand is quiet. Use real iconography (Lucide line icons) where a visual cue is needed.

### Exemplars

**Hero, homepage**
> Pressed slowly, on wood. \
> *The way your grandmother's kitchen smelled.*

**Product card subtitle (Sesame)**
> Erode district · Single-origin · Pressed Nov 2025

**Add-to-cart confirmation**
> Added to your basket. Free shipping on orders over ₹999.

**Empty cart**
> Your basket is waiting. \
> Start with the *Sesame*, if you're new here.

**Avoid**
- ❌ "Discover our amazing range of premium oils!"
- ❌ "Healthy. Pure. Natural." (three-word slogans, generic)
- ❌ "🌿 100% organic 🌿"
- ❌ "Click here to learn more"

**Prefer**
- ✅ "From a wooden press in Erode, to your kitchen in eight days."
- ✅ "Unrefined. Unfiltered. Bottled within 72 hours of pressing."
- ✅ "Read the harvest notes →"

## VISUAL FOUNDATIONS

### Colour — earthy, warm, restrained
The palette is a **kitchen-shelf palette**. Imagine a cream wall, a dark forest-green tin, a brass measuring spoon, a wooden spatula. Nothing is saturated. Nothing fluoresces.

- **Forest greens** are the primary voice — the colour of curry leaves, holy basil, neem. Used for headings, primary CTAs, the wordmark, the trust strip.
- **Mustard / turmeric** is the single accent that draws the eye. Used sparingly: a small badge, an underline, a hover state on a link. Never a button background unless it's a hero CTA.
- **Cream** is the page. Bright white is forbidden — too clinical. We sit at `#FBF7EC`–`#F5EFE0`, the colour of unbleached paper.
- **Wood brown** is structural — borders, dividers, the footer.
- **Terracotta** appears once or twice per page, max — a hero accent, an editorial pull-quote highlight.

Pure white (`#FFFFFF`) is banned. Pure black (`#000000`) is banned. Everything is warm-shifted.

### Typography — serif heart, sans body
- **Display:** Cormorant Garamond. Light to regular weight, often used in italic at large sizes for poetry. *Substituted from Google Fonts — if there's a licensed serif (e.g. Tiempos, GT Sectra), swap it in.*
- **Body:** Manrope. Geometric, friendly, modern, optical-size-aware.
- **Mono:** DM Mono. For batch numbers, harvest dates, pressed-on stamps.

**Pairing rule:** display serif lives at fs-2xl and above (≥32 px). Below that it becomes hard to read at this tracking. Sans handles everything from `lead` down.

**Tracking:** display gets tight (`-0.02em`). Eyebrows and labels get *very* wide (`0.12em`) — this is the only visual nod to traditional inscriptional letter-spacing.

### Spacing — generous, never tight
4-pt rhythm. Sections breathe at `--sp-9` (96 px) on desktop, `--sp-7` (48 px) on mobile. Cards have 24–32 px interior padding. Buttons are 44 px minimum tall (touch target). Two adjacent text blocks always have at least 16 px between them.

### Backgrounds
- **Page:** flat cream (`--cream-200`). No gradients on the page itself.
- **Hero:** flat cream OR full-bleed editorial photograph (oil in a bottle, hand pouring, a wood-press wheel).
- **Section dividers:** sometimes a single hairline rule in `--wood-300`, sometimes a 1-line italic serif word ("nature." or "tradition."), sometimes nothing.
- **Subtle gradients** allowed only as protection scrims over photography (cream → transparent at the top, ink → transparent at the bottom).
- **No repeating patterns. No noise textures. No mandalas. No paisley.** The brand resists Indian-ethnic clichés.

### Animation
- **Default easing:** `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — a soft out-curve, no bounce.
- **Default duration:** 240 ms for hovers, 420 ms for page-level reveals.
- **Hovers:** colour shift only (darker green for primary, mustard underline for links). **No scale on hover for buttons.** Cards can lift by 2px max — never more.
- **Press:** opacity drop to 0.92 + a 1-px translate down. No big "squish".
- **Page transitions:** 200 ms cross-fade. No slide-ins.
- **Cart drawer:** slide from right, 320 ms, ease-out. Scrim fades at 200 ms.

### Borders
Borders are rare and thin. When used: `1px solid var(--border)` (a faint cream-tinted hairline). Cards prefer **shadow over border** — but inputs always get a 1-px border (cream-400 → wood-500 on focus).

### Shadows — soft, warm-tinted
The shadow ink is `rgba(43, 28, 15, …)` — a deep wood brown, not grey or black. This is what makes the system feel warm at depth. Layers: `xs · sm · md · lg · xl`. Cards default to `--sh-sm`; modals to `--sh-lg`; drawers to `--sh-xl`. No coloured shadows. No long blur radii on small elements.

### Corner radii
- Buttons: `--r-md` (12 px).
- Inputs: `--r-md`.
- Cards: `--r-lg` (18 px).
- Modals / drawers: `--r-xl` (28 px) on the outward edge only.
- Pills (chips, badges): `--r-pill`.
- Images in cards: same radius as the card it sits in.

### Cards
Default card:
```
background: var(--bg-elevated);   /* cream-100 */
border-radius: var(--r-lg);
box-shadow: var(--sh-sm);
padding: 24px;
border: none;                     /* never a border *and* a shadow */
transition: box-shadow var(--d-med) var(--ease-out),
            transform   var(--d-med) var(--ease-out);
```
On hover: `box-shadow: var(--sh-md); transform: translateY(-2px);`

### Transparency & blur
- **Backdrop blur** only on the sticky header when the page is scrolled (`backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(1.2);` over a `rgba(251, 247, 236, 0.78)` cream).
- **Image protection gradients** only when text overlays photography. Never decorative blur.
- **Glassmorphism is banned.**

### Imagery — the look of the photography
- **Warm, daylight, slightly desaturated.** No magic-hour orange wash. No flat-lay studio gloss.
- **A faint film grain** at 6–10% on hero imagery is welcome.
- **Subjects:** oil in dark amber glass, a hand pouring, a wood-press wheel mid-turn, a banana-leaf thali, raw ingredients (sesame seeds spilled on jute), the founder, the farm.
- **No stock photography.** No people-in-suits. No floating product on white.
- **Aspect ratios:** 4:5 portrait for product, 16:9 or 21:9 for hero, 1:1 for editorial.

### Layout rules
- **Container width** is 1240 px max. Editorial content narrows to 760 px.
- **Sticky elements:** header (always), mobile bottom-nav (mobile only), in-cart "Place order" button (mobile checkout only).
- **Asymmetry encouraged** in editorial sections — a left-aligned headline with a 60% column of text, image floating right.

## ICONOGRAPHY

The brand uses **Lucide** (line icons, 1.5-stroke, rounded line caps) loaded from CDN. Lucide is chosen because:
- Strokes are thin enough not to fight the elegant serifs.
- Rounded caps match our soft radii.
- Filled variants exist where we need them (cart badge, favourite).

**Stroke weight:** 1.5 px at 20–24 px size, 1.75 px at 32 px+.
**Default size:** 20 px in nav, 16 px inline, 24 px in cards.
**Colour:** inherits `currentColor` — usually `--fg` or `--fg-muted`.

No icon should ever appear *without a label* in nav or CTAs (accessibility). Icon-only buttons must include `aria-label` and an inline tooltip.

**Banned in this brand:**
- ❌ Emoji of any kind.
- ❌ Coloured pictographic illustrations as icons (e.g. a green leaf with a yellow sun).
- ❌ Material Icons (too geometric, too cool-toned).
- ❌ Heroicons "solid" (too heavy for body text).
- ❌ Unicode glyphs as icons (★ ✓ etc) — use the Lucide equivalent.

**Logo & wordmark:**
- Primary wordmark: `assets/logo-wordmark.svg` — serif "Punyakoti" with "Taila" set smaller below in caps.
- Monogram: `assets/logo-monogram.svg` — a stylised "P·T" inside a circle, used for the favicon, the bottle cap, and the mobile header.
- Cow silhouette (Punyakoti motif): `assets/cow-mark.svg` — used as a watermark only, never as the primary mark.

These three marks are **original work for this system**. If you have existing logo files, replace them and the rest of the system will inherit cleanly.

Create an animated video or motion design piece rendered as an HTML page. Build a timeline-based animation with smooth transitions. Design frame-by-frame sequences with playback controls (play/pause, scrubber). Focus on visual storytelling with the Anthropic brand palette. Export-ready at a fixed aspect ratio (16:9 or 9:16). If you need to know the position of an element (eg to move a cursor or character between elements) use refs to grab the position.

START by calling `copy_starter_component` with `kind: "animations.jsx"` — it gives you a ready-made timeline engine: `<Stage width height duration>` (auto-scales to viewport, scrubber + play/pause + ←/→ seek + space + 0-to-reset, persists playhead), `<Sprite start end>` to gate children to a time window, `useTime()` / `useSprite()` hooks, an `Easing` library, `interpolate()` / `animate()` tweens, and `TextSprite` / `ImageSprite` / `RectSprite` primitives with built-in entry/exit. Animations are complex code! Make reusable JSX components for each visual element and each scene. Invest in tweaking the timeline iteratively.

Animation tips:
- Storytelling is KEY! Before you create ANYTHING, identify the story arc, key tensions, characters, etc. Align on the message you want to convey. Run it by the user.
- Use good animation principles... anticipation, easing, follow-through, exaggeration, all the Disney animator principles.
- Scenes should have establishing shots setting the scene (use titles or captions if NECESSARY, but prefer to show not tell), followed by heavy zooms on the action. (either hard cuts, or ken-burns-style zooms, or mouse-follows.) Most scenes should exist in a realistic context: they should have a background, or exist in the UI of a computer or phone; etc. Elements should generally not float in the aether.
- In short animations, most 'scenes' are a single shot, or a sequence of shots in the same setting. Scenes may be slides (e.g. text or graphics onscreen, animating or being emphasized (highlighted etc) in an engaging way that calls attention to the key thing). Decide what the shot is going to be. Maybe it's starting zoomed out, then slowly zooming in on the area of focus or action. Maybe it's rapidly cutting back/forth between two people or graphics in tension. Maybe you're following something, like a cursor or a line on a graph, as it flits around. Be creative!
- Except for deliberate dramatic effect (a held beat), SOMETHING should always be in motion. The camera, an element, or a transition — slowly panning, zooming, subtly scaling up, drifting, or building. A truly static frame reads as a bug. Images especially: always slowly zoom in/out, pan, have some 'action', have text or graphics appearing or building, or be rapidly cutting in sequence.
- Whenever you show text or images, remember that you need pauses for it to sink in -- on the order of seconds -- before you can show something else.

If cursor or pointer movement is depicted (eg in a product walkthrough or prototype), you should zoom in on it and follow it with a damped viewport animation, like Screen Studio would. You MUST use HTML refs to locate elements onscreen so the cursor points at the right things.

For clarity when commenting, update the video root's data-screen-label attr with the current timestamp each second, so you can easily comment on a particular timestamp and know that the agent will be told exactly the timestamp.

### Design Thinking

Before coding, understand the context and commit to a BOLD aesthetic direction:
- **Purpose**: What problem does this interface solve? Who uses it?
- **Tone**: Pick an extreme: brutally minimal, maximalist chaos, retro-futuristic, organic/natural, luxury/refined, playful/toy-like, editorial/magazine, brutalist/raw, art deco/geometric, soft/pastel, industrial/utilitarian, etc. Use these for inspiration but design one that is true to the aesthetic direction.
- **Differentiation**: What makes this UNFORGETTABLE? What's the one thing someone will remember?

Choose a clear conceptual direction and execute it with precision. Bold maximalism and refined minimalism both work — the key is intentionality, not intensity.

### Aesthetics Guidelines

- **Typography**: Choose fonts that are beautiful, unique, and interesting. Avoid generic fonts like Arial and Inter; opt for distinctive, characterful choices. Pair a distinctive display font with a refined body font.
- **Color & Theme**: Commit to a cohesive aesthetic. Use CSS variables for consistency. Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes.
- **Motion**: Use animations for effects and micro-interactions. Prioritize CSS-only solutions for HTML. Focus on high-impact moments: one well-orchestrated page load with staggered reveals creates more delight than scattered micro-interactions.
- **Spatial Composition**: Unexpected layouts. Asymmetry. Overlap. Diagonal flow. Grid-breaking elements. Generous negative space OR controlled density.
- **Backgrounds & Visual Details**: Create atmosphere and depth rather than defaulting to solid colors. Gradient meshes, noise textures, geometric patterns, layered transparencies, dramatic shadows, decorative borders, grain overlays.

Vary between light and dark themes, different fonts, different aesthetics. NEVER converge on the same choices across generations.

Match implementation complexity to the aesthetic vision. Maximalist designs need elaborate animations and effects. Minimalist designs need restraint, precision, and careful attention to spacing and subtle details.

# Save as PDF

Export the current HTML design as a print-friendly HTML file optimized for PDF export.

## Steps

1. **Read the current HTML design file** to understand its structure and content.

2. **Create a print-ready HTML file**. The print file path is the source path with `-print` inserted before the extension — same directory, same basename. If the source is `slides/deck.html`, write `slides/deck-print.html`; if the source is `web/index.html`, write `web/index-print.html`. **Do NOT** use the deck title or project name as the filename, and **do NOT** write to the project root if the source is in a subdirectory — any change in directory depth breaks every relative URL (`@font-face` `src: url(...)`, `<img src>`, `<link href>`, CSS `background: url(...)`) and the print tab shows missing images and system-font fallbacks.

   - Add `@media print` styles with appropriate rules:
     - `@page { size: landscape; margin: 0.5cm; }` for 16:9 slide-like proportions
     - Remove background colors that won't print by default (or use `-webkit-print-color-adjust: exact` to force them)
   - Use CSS page break properties:
     - `break-before: page` to start new pages
     - `break-inside: avoid` to prevent splitting elements across pages
     - `break-after: page` where appropriate
   - Convert scroll-based or interactive layouts to static paged layouts
   - Remove hover states, animations, transitions, and `overflow: hidden` clipping
   - Remove any JavaScript interactivity that doesn't make sense in print
   - Preserve all visual content — images, SVGs, colors, typography

If using unmodified deck-stage.js, your deck should already be print-ready, so you can just copy the file and add the auto-print script!

3. **Test the file** by showing it with `show_html`, then make sure there are no JS errors. No need to screenshot unless asked.

4. **Add the auto-print script** to the file after verifying it looks correct. This should call `window.print()`, but not before making sure the layout is ready (ie fonts and JS have loaded). Make sure your code waits until these conditions are met:
- All fonts are loaded
- If using Babel JSX transforms, make sure this code does not execute until those are parsed (e.g. place it within a JSX script.)
- Add a 500ms delay just to be safe
- Use your judgement; there may be other things to wait for besides these depending on the page!

5. **Call the `open_for_print` tool** with the project-relative path to the print-ready file.

## Important Notes

- The goal is a file that looks great when saved as PDF via the browser's print dialog
- Maintain visual fidelity — the PDF should look as close to the original design as possible
- For slide decks or multi-section designs, each slide/section should be on its own page
- The `-print.html` is plumbing for the print tab, not a deliverable — `open_for_print` is the only delivery step. Do NOT `present_fs_item_for_download` it; its relative asset paths only resolve via the project file server and break when opened standalone.
